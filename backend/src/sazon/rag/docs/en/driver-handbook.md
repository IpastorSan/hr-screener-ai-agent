# Driver handbook

This handbook explains how the Grupo Sazón delivery team works in Spain and Mexico. Grupo Sazón delivers orders from its own restaurants with its own team, with no delivery platform in between, and each driver always works from the same kitchen.

## The role

The driver picks up orders in the kitchen, checks that they are complete and delivers them to customers within the kitchen's delivery area, usually within a radius of about 4 km. No previous experience is needed. The work is organised through the Grupo Sazón delivery app, which is free and runs on any smartphone with data. The app assigns orders in the order they leave the kitchen, shows the route and lets you mark each delivery.

Each kitchen has a shift manager, who is the person to ask about any question, schedule change or incident.

## A typical shift

There are lunch and dinner shifts. In Spain the lunch shift runs from 12:30 to 16:30 and the dinner shift from 19:30 to 23:30. In Mexico the lunch shift runs from 13:00 to 17:00 and the dinner shift from 19:00 to 23:00. The schedule is published every week based on the availability each person gives, and weekends, the busiest days, are part of almost every schedule.

At the start of the shift you clock in on the app, collect the company motorbike and check it in a couple of minutes: lights, brakes, tyre pressure and fuel. Then you wait at the kitchen while orders are prepared. Two to three deliveries an hour at peak time is normal. At the end you return the motorbike with more than half a tank, leave the insulated backpack clean and clock out.

The first shift is paid training: you ride alongside someone from the team, who shows you the area, the app and how to hand over orders.

## Safety on the road

Safety comes before speed. No order justifies running a red light, riding on the pavement or using your phone while riding. Delivery times are calculated assuming calm riding, and nobody is penalised for arriving late because of traffic or weather.

In heavy rain, wind or hail the shift manager can shrink the area or pause deliveries. If someone does not feel safe going out, they tell the manager and stay in.

## Helmet and gear

Company motorbikes come with an approved helmet and gloves. With your own motorbike you can use your own helmet if it is approved. Grupo Sazón provides the uniform, a waterproof jacket and the insulated backpack free of charge; they are returned when employment ends. The helmet is always fastened, even on short trips.

## Dealing with customers

The driver is the face of Grupo Sazón at every customer's door. Greet the customer, confirm the name on the order and hand it over with the bag sealed. Never go inside a customer's home. If a customer asks for something that cannot be done, such as carrying the order up stairs with no access or waiting a long time, explain politely and let the kitchen know.

## Tips

Drivers keep all their tips. Card tips left in the app are added to the payslip; cash tips are kept by the driver the same day. Grupo Sazón takes nothing from tips.

## Incidents during a delivery

If the customer does not answer, call them from the app and wait up to 10 minutes at the address; then tell the kitchen, which decides whether the order comes back.

If you get a flat tyre or the company motorbike breaks down, park somewhere safe and tell the shift manager, who arranges the pickup. With your own motorbike, repairs are the owner's responsibility.

If there is an accident, first get to safety and call emergency services if anyone is hurt (112 in Spain, 911 in Mexico). Then tell the shift manager, who helps with the report. Accidents during a shift, and on the way between home and the kitchen, are work accidents and are covered by Social Security in Spain and by the IMSS in Mexico from day one.
